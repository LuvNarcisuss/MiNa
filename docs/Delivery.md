## App and cloud

- MiNa uses SwiftUI screens, a native navigation stack and cloud-hosted artwork on iPhones running iOS 16.0 or newer. The app, scheme and target are named MiNa. The bundle identifier is com.mina.bedtime. The native URLSession client uses the existing service. Device settings retain their account-specific directories. User media is served from private Storage. Release signing and App Store products require owner-provided identifiers.
- The iPhone client uses the Tokyo project owned by Thingtill Org, reference `weieitgfketnpvvwvfpa`. The Gmail-linked Tokyo project `nojpfyqctfwdshqnzudp` is the migration source. The owner explicitly approved permanent removal of Toli. The dashboard acknowledged deletion, and the organization listing subsequently contained MiNa and Sese.
- The entry screen uses Supabase email and password authentication. The existing private `auth.users.email` and `auth.users.encrypted_password` columns hold the email and salted password hash. The entry screen offers Sign in and Create account. Administrators can create confirmed email/password accounts in Authentication > Users. Cold launch requires a fresh sign-in. Password sessions are memory-only, and an unlinked device account retains its original Keychain item. On November 24, 2026, schema inspection confirmed both columns and the public Auth settings reported email registration enabled with email confirmation required. On November 25, 2026, the company project disabled Confirm email. Its public Auth settings returned `external.email: true`, `disable_signup: false` and `mailer_autoconfirm: true`. After Supabase confirms registration, the app discards the registration session and shows an account creation alert. Tapping OK returns to sign-in. The updated flow passed Swift syntax parsing. End-to-end registration on a device remains untested.
- The deployed database covers profiles, visibility, follows, blocks, reports, posts, dreams, private media, comments, reactions, messages, calling, room presence, seats, audio signaling, gifts, wallet records and support. The economy deployment recorded here predates the wallet purchase migration. The app uses signed media URLs and user-scoped requests.
- Central plus opens AI Companion. The three illustrated topic cards work without a model. After the user agrees to share messages, the conversation requests Groq replies through the authenticated companion-chat function. The service provides renewable, free 60-minute sessions and a ten-minute warning. No third-party inference key is embedded.
- Profile feedback submits to a protected support table. The owner requested that the public support email stay blank. The in-app agreement text and publicly readable PDFs use the support form as the contact channel. Public GET responses for both PDFs matched the checked-in documents byte for byte.
- Room audio and one-to-one calls use WebRTC and authenticated, participant-scoped signaling. A foreground receiver can answer or decline. Production TURN configuration, background incoming-call notifications and physical-device audio acceptance remain outstanding.
- Twenty gifts and VIP0 through VIP12 are available in the interface. The current wallet source uses StoreKit 1 and validates all seven consumables on the device. The account's device ledger records purchase credits, local store items and device-funded spending. Shared gift fulfillment uses room and participant checks and attested device contributions. The earlier remote purchase verifier is recorded in the historical deployment section below. See [wallet release dependencies and verification](Wallet.md).
